## Observation retention

The shared request-scope policy keeps the current maximum at **3,600 elapsed seconds**. A separate `latest_reported` object can retain a valid supplied observation through **86,400 elapsed seconds**, inclusive. This 24-hour background limit is an application retention choice, not an NWS statement of representativeness. Reports beyond it are excluded from usable facts and marked `outside_background_retention`. Observation age and source/cache age are evaluated independently. A source snapshot must have a valid fetch timestamp no more than one hour old, with the existing five-minute future-clock tolerance; stale/unavailable sources cannot become latest-reported evidence.

Both current and background paths require a configured NWS station and exact latest-observation URL, matching station identity, valid observation timestamp, valid coordinates, and coordinates within 5 km of the configured station. The coordinate tolerance is an application sanity check, not site applicability. Background fields require finite numeric values and supplied units; field quality flags are retained without upgrading them to a quality guarantee. Malformed or missing fields do not become values. Raw payloads remain inspectable. Invalid times, failed retrievals, bad station identity/coordinates, stale source snapshots, missing values and expired background retention retain different metadata dispositions.

The supplied KFRG case now retains the 16:00 UTC / 11:00 EST reading at receipt 17:07:10 UTC / 12:07:10 EST: **4,030 seconds (67m 10s)** old, zero seconds of source/cache age. Wind, gusts, direction, units and source flags survive in `latest_reported`; `observation.fields` remains empty. The bounded answer labels it “Latest reported; outside the current freshness window.” It is a different answer-record kind from a current observation and cannot take the current-observation shortcut. No continuous historical coverage, future condition or operational all-clear follows from it.

## Geographic relationships

Explicitly selected/named stations never receive a silent replacement. For non-station geometry, select the nearest **configured** station to each supplied vertex within **50 km**, retaining the union of these station identities. Ties use station ID. Unavailable nearest records remain gaps; a farther record is not substituted. Distance metadata gives minimum/maximum **vertex distances**, not exact nearest distance to a line or polygon. Point selections have a single distance. Line and area selections keep their full geometry; station references do not claim coverage between vertices, across interiors, or on-site representativeness. Sparse/missing geometry yields unresolved coverage.

A station/feature name or regional label cannot establish forecast applicability. Selected-site forecasts require containment of all supplied geometry in the source's GeoJSON footprint before time-window selection supplies values. This increment supports simple, convex, hole-free Polygon cells through 512 positions. It rejects self-intersection and degenerate edges; antimeridian-spanning, concave, holed, multipart, missing and point-only forecast footprints remain unresolved rather than receiving a centroid approximation. Convexity establishes containment of connecting segments and interiors as well as vertices. Outside/unsupported forecasts provide a coverage gap and no site values. This conservative boundary deliberately leaves some usable future evidence unresolved until a richer footprint resolver is implemented.

Current observations, hourly/grid segments within 24 elapsed hours and later point periods retain the existing separate time-window rules. A failed footprint or missing current report cannot be replaced by an unrelated forecast. Source geometry remains an attributed client snapshot, not an independently verified footprint.

Request-local source IDs, claim IDs and answer-record links retain source identity/URL, location relationship, observation/fetch/request times, selected forecast interval and current-versus-latest-reported disposition. Every usable bounded answer record links to a source and claim. A provenance link is not proof of geographic applicability; regional KB and document assertions stay unresolved where no separate support establishes location/time relevance. Existing operational-record checks still control procedures, thresholds and gauge links.

The registry indexes weather, CO-OPS, complete uploads and all enabled context sections, including alerts, loaded layers and KB. Exact UTF-16 context spans work across JS/Python even with emoji. Full context/uploads remain in the existing source viewer and provider input. Complete original weather/CO-OPS snapshots are also retained under `evidence_bundle.raw_sources` in the existing collapsible request metadata and history. Rejected observations remain raw sources, not usable claims. Alert/layer/KB context is indexed for review; this increment does not promote unparsed source text into validated local facts or interpret custom document prose.

The model-facing scope includes a compact bundle inventory; complete eligible weather and its relationships remain in WINDOW MATCHES/navigation, with full source context and uploads following. Serializing the complete bundle again in the prompt duplicated forecast data and broke an existing 96 KiB regression request during development; that duplicate serialization was removed. No enabled source material or upload was shortened, and prompt limits were not raised.
